In May 2024 someone asked r/sheffield a simple question: what's the most famous piece of street art in the city? The answers mixed the famous with personal favourites. Murals don't last forever, so we've checked which of them you can still see. A few have gone, and we say so below.

### I Love You Will U Marry Me

The most common answer, and one commenter argued the story is what makes it art. The words were painted in April 2001 on a bridge high up on the Park Hill flats, as a proposal. The message was kept when the estate was refurbished and picked out in pink neon in 2011. It was taken down during maintenance work in 2021, and put back in 2022. One commenter who grew up on Park Hill said they knew the people involved. Another pointed out that the musical about the estate has carried it to a much wider audience.

### The Steelworker

Several commenters went for the steelworker made of bricks on Castle Street, which one said has been there for donkey's years. Paul Waplington's brick mosaic has been there since 1986. Street Art Sheffield says it was modelled on a real steelworker, but the eyes came out much bigger to suit the size of the bricks. One commenter said a friend's dad was one of the bricklayers.

### Pete McKee

Several people named the whippet with a packet of biscuits stuck on its face. He's called Frank, and he's a Pete McKee, painted on the side of Kelham Arcade on Burton Road in 2016. The biscuits are Hobnobs. McKee had plenty of fans in the thread, though not everyone was one.

His other famous wall is The Snog, the elderly couple kissing on the side of Fagan's pub on Broad Lane. It was lost in May 2024 when the wall had to be re-rendered, and McKee came back and repainted it the same month.

### Kid Acne

Kid Acne's slogans came up more than once, with "Tha knows" and "That'll learn 'em" quoted fondly. His work turns up all over the city. One you can find near the Park Hill bridge is "Unemployed Gods", painted on a wall along South Street.

### Phlegm

"Basically everything Phlegm does is incredible," said one commenter. The Phlegm that came up in the thread was the one off Ecclesall Road, on Snuff Mill Lane, painted in 2016 for the Feature Walls festival. It's gone. In 2023 the wall needed structural repairs and the mural was lost. A new mural celebrating Sheffield FC went up there later that year, and not everyone in the thread was happy about it.

If you want to see a Phlegm now, there's a big one on Eldon Street, painted in 2025.

### Gone, but still talked about

Trik9's David Bowie on Division Street, painted after Bowie died in 2016, was remembered mostly because commenters thought it didn't look much like him. It's no longer there. Nor is the tall tribute to Harry Brearley, the inventor of stainless steel, on the side of the Howard Hotel on Howard Street. One commenter said simply that he is missed.
