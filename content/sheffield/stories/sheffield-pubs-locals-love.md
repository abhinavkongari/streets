Two questions on r/sheffield at the end of 2025 got to the heart of what makes a good pub. In November, someone coming home from a year of travelling wanted a dimly lit pub with a crackling fire and comfortable chairs. In December, a group of friends who had just arrived in the city asked for the best pubs, as long as they weren't rowdy. The same names came up in both.

The Fat Cat and the Sheffield Tap came up too, but they already have their own pages on Streets. Here are the rest.

### The Irish Triangle

Three pubs a couple of minutes apart, just off West Street, which one commenter called the Irish Triangle. One commenter called them some of the last real traditional pubs in town.

**The Grapes**, on Trippet Lane, was named more than any other pub. It's an Irish pub with regular live music, and it's where the Arctic Monkeys played their first gig, in 2003. As of the end of 2025 it was still cash only, much to one regular's annoyance.

**Dog & Partridge** is about three doors down. People liked the close tables and the good atmosphere, and it was recommended again and again.

**Fagan's**, round the corner on Broad Lane, got the most enthusiastic single vote of either thread. One commenter said its gig nights feel tight and cosy. Look out for Pete McKee's mural The Snog on the side wall; McKee has said Fagan's was his pub.

### Towards Kelham Island

From the Triangle, several people's advice was to head on to Kelham Island. **Shakespeare's** on Gibraltar Street turned up on almost every list. So did the **Kelham Island Tavern** on Russell Street, which became the first pub to be named CAMRA's National Pub of the Year two years running, in 2008 and 2009. It has a small beer garden with palm trees.

If you're still going, one regular's crawl carries on to **the Gardeners Rest** on Neepsend Lane. They admitted it's a bit of a trek, but it was on other people's lists too.

### Firelight and dark corners

For the person in search of a fire, the first answer was **Hallamshire House** on Commonside. Fans said it's the one, though it's less dim on a Saturday night than on a weeknight, and one person recommended the rustic basement.

**The Bath Hotel** on Victoria Street is dimly lit, though one commenter didn't think it has a fire. It's worth a visit for the room alone: the interior dates from a 1931 remodel, the building is Grade II listed, and the pub is on CAMRA's National Inventory of Historic Pub Interiors.

**The Stags Head** on Psalter Lane has a fire too, and another commenter backed it as a great shout.
