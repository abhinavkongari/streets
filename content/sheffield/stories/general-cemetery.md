Walk through the Egyptian-style gatehouse on Cemetery Road and the city goes quiet. Sheffield General Cemetery opened in 1836 on a hillside above the Porter Brook, and it was designed as a landscape as much as a burial ground. Robert Marnock, who laid out the Botanical Gardens, planned the planting.

It stopped taking burials in the late twentieth century and was slowly taken over by trees, ivy and wildlife. Today it's a listed park looked after by a charitable trust. You can follow paths between leaning headstones, find the graves of some of the people who built Victorian Sheffield, and hear the birds.

The trust runs guided tours and events through the year. It's free to walk in at any time the gates are open.
