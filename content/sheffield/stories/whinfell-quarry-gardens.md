When someone posted to r/sheffield in June 2026 after their first visit to Whinfell Quarry Gardens, the replies filled up with people who already loved it, and one of them begged everyone to stop telling people. It's small and a little out of the way, just past Ecclesall Woods, and it doesn't look like anywhere else in the city. The poster said the giant redwoods made them think of California, and the layout reminded them of a Chinese garden. One commenter summed it up: "It's like a tiny paradise."

The garden fills two disused flagstone quarries. In the late 1890s the steel industrialist Samuel Doncaster leased the site and began turning it into the garden for his new house, Whinfell House, planting rare trees and shrubs, including redwoods, Japanese acers, bamboo and rhododendrons, around a waterfall and a chain of pools. In the smaller quarry, the plant collector Clarence Elliott later designed a limestone rock garden. In 1968 the Neill family company gave the garden to the city as a memorial to Sir Frederick Neill, who had owned it after Doncaster. The house burned down in 1971, but the garden has stayed a public park, and it is now listed at Grade II on the national register of historic parks and gardens.

Climb to the seat at the top of the big quarry for the view down over the pools.

**Good to know:** the main entrance is on Ecclesall Road South, and one visitor found space to park on the road just outside. Commenters like to add it to the end of a walk through Ecclesall Woods, about ten minutes away on foot. A gate at the top of the garden opens onto Fenney Lane, an old sunken lane you can follow up to Whirlow Farm for a short round walk. Whirlow Brook Park is right next door too.
