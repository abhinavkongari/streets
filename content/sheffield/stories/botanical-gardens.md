The Botanical Gardens opened in 1836, laid out by the landscape gardener Robert Marnock. Almost two hundred years later they're still one of the most peaceful places in the city.

The centrepiece is the row of glass pavilions at the top of the lawn. They fell into disrepair in the late twentieth century and were restored in the early 2000s. Inside you'll find plants from climates a long way from South Yorkshire.

Outside there are themed gardens, a bear pit left over from the Victorian gardens (the bears are long gone), and a sloping lawn that fills up with picnics whenever the sun appears.

Entry is free. It's an easy walk from the university and from Ecclesall Road.
